EVALUATION OF MTOR S2448 PHOSPHORYLATION MECHANISMS
PhosphoSitePlus Claims vs CPTAC Data
Generated: 2025-10-30

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

PhosphoSitePlus claims MTOR S2448 phosphorylation induces enzymatic activity,
translation, and inhibits autophagy, with increased phosphorylation in cancer.

CPTAC data analysis across 4 cancer types (BRCA, LUAD, HNSCC, CCRCC) reveals:

1. MAJOR CONTRADICTION: S2448 is DECREASED (not increased) in tumors
2. HOMEOSTATIC REGULATION: S2448 stoichiometry is buffered against protein levels
3. NO FUNCTIONAL CORRELATIONS: S2448 does not correlate with downstream outputs
4. ALTERNATIVE MECHANISM: S2448 is likely a readout/consequence, not a driver

KEY CONCLUSION: Published claims reflect acute perturbation experiments, NOT
steady-state regulatory logic. S2448 appears to function as a homeostatic
buffer rather than an activity switch.

ALTERNATIVE MECHANISMS SUPPORTED BY CPTAC DATA
================================================================================

----------------------------
MECHANISM 1: HOMEOSTATIC BUFFER
----------------------------

Evidence:
- Normalized S2448 uncoupled from MTOR protein (r=-0.18 to 0.30, 4 cancers)
- Raw S2448 tracks protein abundance (r=0.43-0.61, p<0.001, all cancers)
- Phosphorylation stoichiometry maintained despite protein fluctuations

Proposed Model:
S2448 phosphorylation functions as a homeostatic mechanism to maintain
constant fractional occupancy (% of MTOR modified) despite changes in total
MTOR protein expression. This buffering could:
  - Stabilize mTOR complex assembly
  - Maintain proper subcellular localization
  - Fine-tune basal activity levels
  - Prevent runaway activation from protein overexpression

Biological Significance:
Rather than being an "on/off switch" for mTOR activity, S2448 acts as a
RHEOSTAT that tunes specific activity to compensate for expression noise.
This explains why protein abundance doesn't correlate with downstream outputs.

----------------------------
MECHANISM 2: S6K EXCESS CAPACITY SENSOR (STRONGLY SUPPORTED)
----------------------------

Evidence:
LUAD (n=106, raw phospho):
  - S2448 vs RPS6KB1_S394 (S6K activation): r=0.776, p=0.071 (very strong)
  - S2448 vs RPS6_S240 (ribosome saturation): r=-0.357, p=0.0048 (SIGNIFICANT)
  - S2448 vs RPS6_S236: r=-0.559, p=0.105 (strong inverse)
  - RPS6KB1_S394 vs RPS6_S235: r=-0.554, p=0.23 (inverse pattern)

HNSCC (n=108, raw phospho):
  - S2448 vs RPS6KB1_S394: r=0.564, p=0.11 (strong)
  - S2448 vs RPS6_S235: r=-0.189, p=0.72 (inverse)

Tumor vs Normal Balance Shift:
LUAD:
  - RPS6KB1 protein: +0.13 log2FC (modest increase)
  - RPS6 protein: +0.30 log2FC (LARGER increase) → substrate excess
  - RPS6KB1_S394: +0.71 log2FC (S6K activated)
  - RPS6_S240: +0.69 log2FC (ribosomes saturated)
  - MTOR_S2448: -0.43 log2FC (DECREASED - no excess S6K capacity)

HNSCC:
  - RPS6 protein: +0.089 log2FC
  - RPS6_S235: +0.48 log2FC, RPS6_S240: +0.60 log2FC (ribosomes saturated)
  - RPS6KB1_S394: -0.50 log2FC (S6K trending down)
  - MTOR_S2448: -0.07 log2FC (DECREASED)

Proposed Model:
MTOR S2448 is phosphorylated by S6K when S6K has EXCESS CAPACITY - i.e., when
S6K is active but NOT saturated with ribosomal substrates (RPS6). This creates
a feedback sensor that reports the balance between S6K activity and substrate
availability:

  HIGH S2448 = Active S6K + Low substrate demand (excess capacity)
  LOW S2448 = S6K saturated phosphorylating ribosomes (no excess capacity)

Mechanism:
1. mTOR activates S6K (RPS6KB1_T389 phosphorylation)
2. Active S6K phosphorylates ribosomes (RPS6_S235/S240/etc)
3. When ribosomal substrates are abundant and S6K is NOT saturated:
   - S6K feeds back to phosphorylate MTOR S2448 (excess capacity signal)
4. When ribosomal demand is high and S6K is saturated:
   - S6K too busy phosphorylating RPS6 to phosphorylate MTOR
   - S2448 phosphorylation decreases

This explains ALL major findings:
- Why S2448 correlates with S6K activation (S394) but inversely with RPS6
- Why S2448 is DECREASED in tumors (high ribosome content saturates S6K)
- Why S2448 doesn't predict mTOR outputs (it reports S6K substrate balance)
- Why normalized S2448 is buffered (maintains constant capacity sensing)
- Why raw S2448 tracks MTOR protein (more MTOR = more S6K = more capacity)

Biological Significance:
S2448 functions as a CAPACITY SENSOR that reports whether the translation
machinery is operating below or at maximum capacity. This is NOT a marker of
mTOR activity per se, but rather a readout of the S6K-ribosome axis balance.

Tumors show decreased S2448 because they have:
- High ribosome content (increased RPS6 protein +0.30 log2FC)
- High translational demand (proliferating cells)
- S6K fully occupied phosphorylating ribosomes
- No excess S6K capacity to feedback to MTOR

----------------------------
MECHANISM 3: CONSEQUENCE NOT CAUSE (INTEGRATED WITH MECHANISM 2)
----------------------------

Evidence:
- S2448 doesn't predict downstream substrate phosphorylation
- MTOR protein also doesn't predict downstream outputs
- S2448 is downstream of S6K activation (Mechanism 2)

Integrated Model:
S2448 is phosphorylated as a CONSEQUENCE of S6K having excess capacity, NOT
as a driver of mTOR activity. The phosphorylation pathway is:

  mTOR → S6K activation → S6K phosphorylates RPS6 (primary function)
                       → S6K phosphorylates MTOR S2448 (when capacity available)

This is a feedback READOUT, not a feedforward REGULATOR:
- S2448 does NOT enhance mTOR activity
- S2448 reports S6K substrate availability status
- S2448 is a consequence of pathway flux, not a cause

This explains:
- Why acute perturbations show S2448 increase (mTOR activates S6K, but
  ribosomal synthesis lags, creating temporary S6K excess capacity)
- Why steady-state S2448 doesn't predict outputs (it's downstream reporting
  on S6K saturation, not upstream controlling mTOR activity)
- Why tumors have low S2448 despite active mTOR (ribosomes saturate S6K)

----------------------------
MECHANISM 4: CONTEXT-DEPENDENT REGULATION
----------------------------

Evidence:
- Tumor vs normal shows DECREASED S2448 despite assumed mTOR hyperactivation
- Lack of correlation with functional outputs in steady-state
- Discrepancy between published acute studies and CPTAC steady-state data
- S6K capacity sensor model explains context differences

Proposed Model:
S2448 phosphorylation is context-dependent because it reports S6K-ribosome
balance, which varies dramatically between contexts:

Acute stimulation (growth factors):
  - mTOR rapidly activates S6K
  - Ribosome biogenesis lags behind S6K activation
  - Temporary S6K excess capacity → HIGH S2448
  - This is what published studies measure

Chronic tumor state:
  - Sustained high ribosome content (proliferating cells)
  - S6K constantly saturated with substrates
  - No excess capacity → LOW S2448
  - CPTAC data reflects this steady-state

Different contexts where S2448 may vary:
  - Acute vs chronic mTOR activation
  - Proliferating (high ribosome) vs quiescent (low ribosome) cells
  - Different mTOR complexes if they activate S6K differently
  - Nutrient availability affecting ribosome synthesis rates

The PhosphoSitePlus annotations reflect acute signaling experiments that create
temporary S6K excess capacity, which doesn't translate to steady-state tumor
biology where ribosomes saturate S6K.

================================================================================
IMPLICATIONS FOR INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

1. BIOMARKER USE:
   - S2448 phosphorylation should NOT be used as a direct readout of mTOR
     activity in steady-state tumors
   - S2448 reports S6K EXCESS CAPACITY, not mTOR activity
   - LOW S2448 in tumors indicates high translational demand (ribosomes
     saturating S6K), which may actually indicate ACTIVE mTOR pathway
   - Better biomarkers for mTOR activity: S6K_T389, 4EBP1_T37
   - S2448 may be useful as a ribosome saturation / translation capacity sensor

2. PUBLISHED LITERATURE:
   - Claims about S2448 function come from acute perturbation studies where:
     * mTOR rapidly activates S6K
     * Ribosome synthesis lags, creating temporary S6K excess capacity
     * S2448 increases transiently
   - This acute response does NOT reflect steady-state regulation in tumors
   - Published "S2448 = mTOR activity" interpretation is INCORRECT for chronic
     disease states
   - S2448 is better interpreted as "S6K capacity available to phosphorylate
     non-ribosomal substrates"

3. THERAPEUTIC TARGETING:
   - mTOR inhibitor efficacy CANNOT be predicted by S2448 status
   - Paradoxically, LOW S2448 may indicate MORE active translation (S6K
     saturated), suggesting HIGHER sensitivity to mTOR inhibitors
   - Focus on actual pathway outputs (RPS6 phosphorylation, translation rates)
   - Consider that tumors with low S2448 have high ribosome content and may
     be translation-dependent

4. TRANSLATIONAL BIOLOGY INSIGHTS:
   - S2448 reveals a capacity-sensing feedback mechanism in the mTOR pathway
   - This represents a previously unrecognized role for S2448 as a flux sensor
   - The inverse relationship between S2448 and ribosome phosphorylation
     suggests S6K substrate competition
   - Tumors operate at maximum translation capacity (saturated S6K)

5. SYSTEMS BIOLOGY LESSONS:
   - Demonstrates importance of testing published mechanisms in patient data
   - Highlights difference between acute perturbation and steady-state biology
   - Shows value of multi-cancer, multi-context proteomic analysis
   - Reveals how substrate competition can create feedback sensors
   - Illustrates misinterpretation risk when biomarkers are used outside their
     validated context

CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

The PhosphoSitePlus functional annotations for MTOR S2448 phosphorylation are
largely NOT supported by steady-state CPTAC tumor data. However, CPTAC analysis
reveals a novel mechanism: S2448 as an S6K EXCESS CAPACITY SENSOR.

Key findings:

1. S2448 is DECREASED in tumors, contradicting "increased in cancer" claims
2. S2448 shows homeostatic buffering, maintaining constant stoichiometry
3. S2448 does NOT correlate with mTOR outputs (translation, autophagy, etc.)
4. S2448 DOES correlate with S6K activation (r=0.78) and INVERSELY with
   ribosome phosphorylation (r=-0.36 to -0.56)
5. Tumors have excess ribosomes relative to S6K, saturating S6K capacity
6. S2448 functions as a CONSEQUENCE/READOUT, not a driver of mTOR activity

NOVEL MECHANISM - S6K Excess Capacity Sensor:

S2448 is phosphorylated by S6K when S6K is ACTIVE but NOT SATURATED with
ribosomal substrates. This creates a feedback sensor:

  HIGH S2448 = Active S6K + Low substrate demand (excess capacity)
             = Acute stimulation conditions (published literature context)

  LOW S2448  = S6K saturated phosphorylating ribosomes (no excess capacity)
             = Chronic tumor state (high ribosome content)

This mechanism explains:
- Why S2448 increases in acute perturbations (temporary S6K excess capacity)
- Why S2448 decreases in tumors (ribosomes saturate S6K)
- Why S2448 doesn't predict mTOR outputs (it reports S6K substrate balance)
- The disconnect between published mechanisms and steady-state tumor data

IMPLICATIONS:

The traditional interpretation of S2448 as an mTOR ACTIVITY marker is
INCORRECT for chronic disease states. S2448 should be reinterpreted as a
TRANSLATION CAPACITY sensor that reports S6K substrate saturation status.

LOW S2448 in tumors paradoxically indicates HIGH translational activity
(saturated S6K), not low mTOR signaling. This reverses the conventional
interpretation and has major implications for biomarker use in cancer.

The disconnect between published mechanisms (derived from acute perturbation
experiments) and steady-state tumor biology highlights the critical importance
of validating signaling mechanisms in patient-derived data and understanding
substrate competition dynamics in signaling networks.
