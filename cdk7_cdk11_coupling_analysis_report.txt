COUPLING CLASSIFICATION: MODERATE, CANCER-SPECIFIC COUPLING

CDK7 and CDK11 show moderate functional coupling that varies significantly
across cancer types. The strongest coupling is observed at the substrate level,
particularly through CDK1_T161 phosphorylation in HNSCC (r=0.78, p<0.0001).
Protein-level coupling is moderate in 3/7 cancers (HNSCC, LUAD, LSCC), while
direct phosphosite coupling between CDK7 regulatory sites and CDK11B_T595 is
generally weak to absent.

KEY FINDINGS:

1. **Substrate-Level Coupling Dominates**: CDK1_T161 (a CDK7 substrate) shows
   the strongest correlation with CDK11B_T595 across cancers, suggesting
   functional coupling occurs through shared pathway activation rather than
   direct phosphorylation.

2. **Cancer-Specific Patterns**:
   - HNSCC: Very strong substrate coupling (CDK1_T161-CDK11B_T595, r=0.78)
   - LSCC: Strong protein coupling (CDK7-CDK11B, r=0.41, p<0.0001) + moderate
     regulatory site coupling (CDK7_T170-CDK11B_T595, r=0.33, p=0.018)
   - LUAD: Moderate protein coupling (r=0.36, p<0.001)
   - COAD, CCRCC, OVARIAN, PDAC: Weak to absent coupling

3. **Coordinated Protein Upregulation**: CDK7 and CDK11B proteins are
   concordantly upregulated in tumors vs normal in 4/7 cancers (COAD, HNSCC,
   LUAD, LSCC), suggesting transcriptional co-regulation.

4. **Phosphosite Dysregulation is Discordant**: Despite protein concordance,
   phosphorylation stoichiometry shows mixed/discordant patterns, indicating
   independent regulation of CDK7 and CDK11B phosphorylation states.

5. **PhosphoSitePlus Relationship Partially Confirmed**: The direct
   CDK7→CDK11B_T595 phosphorylation observed in vitro (PhosphoSitePlus) shows
   weak correlation in tumors, suggesting this interaction is either:
   - Context-dependent (not active in all tumors)
   - Regulated by additional factors not captured in correlations
   - Occurring transiently or in specific cellular compartments

BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

MECHANISTIC INSIGHTS:

1. **Protein-Level Co-regulation (Transcriptional)**:
   CDK7 and CDK11B proteins are coordinately upregulated in 57% of cancers,
   suggesting shared transcriptional control. This may involve:
   - E2F transcription factors (cell cycle regulation)
   - MYC (proliferation signaling)
   - TP53 loss (loss of cell cycle checkpoints)

   However, this transcriptional coupling does NOT automatically translate to
   phosphorylation coupling, indicating independent post-translational regulation.

2. **Phosphorylation Independence (Post-translational)**:
   Despite protein concordance, phosphorylation stoichiometry is discordant in
   43% of cancers. This indicates:
   - Different upstream kinases control CDK7 vs CDK11B phosphorylation
   - Phosphatase activity may differentially regulate the two kinases
   - Subcellular localization may separate the kinases despite co-expression

   The weak CDK7_T170-CDK11B_T595 correlation (mean r=0.13) suggests the in vitro
   CDK7→CDK11B_T595 phosphorylation observed in PhosphoSitePlus is either:
   - Context-dependent (requires specific co-factors or cellular conditions)
   - Counterbalanced by phosphatases in vivo
   - Occurring transiently or in specific cell cycle phases not captured by
     bulk tumor measurements

3. **Substrate-Level Coupling (Functional)**:
   The strongest coupling observed is CDK1_T161-CDK11B_T595 (mean r=0.37, up to
   r=0.78 in HNSCC). This suggests:
   - CDK7 and CDK11 are activated in the SAME CELLULAR CONTEXTS
   - Shared upstream CAK activity (both are targets of CDK-activating kinases)
   - Coordinated cell cycle progression requiring both CDK1 and CDK11
   - Potential co-localization in specific subcellular compartments

   CDK1_T161 phosphorylation (by CDK7/cyclin H/MAT1 complex) is essential for
   CDK1 activation and mitotic entry. The strong correlation with CDK11B_T595
   suggests CDK11 may play coordinated roles in:
   - Mitotic entry/progression (CDK11^p110 isoform)
   - RNA splicing during mitosis
   - Apoptosis regulation (CDK11^p58 isoform)

4. **Cancer-Specific Mechanisms**:
   The striking difference between HNSCC (r=0.78) and LUAD (r=0.12) for
   CDK1_T161-CDK11B_T595 coupling indicates cancer-type-specific wiring.

   HNSCC may have unique features:
   - Increased reliance on CDK signaling for proliferation
   - Altered cell cycle checkpoint control
   - HPV-driven cell cycle deregulation (in HPV+ HNSCC)
   - Unique spatial organization of CDK complexes

   LSCC (also squamous cell carcinoma) shows similar multi-level coupling,
   suggesting squamous cell carcinomas may have distinct CDK regulation compared
   to adenocarcinomas.

FUNCTIONAL IMPLICATIONS:

1. **Therapeutic Targeting**:
   The strong HNSCC coupling suggests CDK7 and CDK11 may function as a
   coordinated unit in head and neck cancer. Combination therapies targeting
   both kinases may be synergistic.

   CDK7 inhibitors (e.g., THZ1, SY-1365) are in clinical trials. Our data
   suggest HNSCC and LSCC may be particularly responsive if CDK11 is co-targeted.

2. **Biomarker Development**:
   CDK1_T161 phosphorylation could serve as a biomarker for CDK7-CDK11 coupling
   status. High CDK1_T161 in HNSCC may predict sensitivity to dual CDK7/CDK11
   inhibition.

3. **Cancer Subtype Stratification**:
   Squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC, LSCC) show stronger coupling than
   adenocarcinomas (LUAD, COAD, PDAC), suggesting histological subtypes have
   distinct CDK regulatory networks.

COMPARISON TO PHOSPHOSITEPLUS DATA:

PhosphoSitePlus Prediction:
  - CDK7 directly phosphorylates CDK11B at T595 (in vitro evidence)
  - CDK7 regulatory sites: T170 (activation), S164 (regulation)

CPTAC Tumor Data:
  - CDK7_T170 - CDK11B_T595: Mean r=0.13 (WEAK)
  - CDK7_S164 - CDK11B_T595: Mean r=0.01 (ABSENT)
  - CDK7_protein - CDK11B_T595: Mean r=-0.06 (SLIGHTLY NEGATIVE)

CONCLUSION: The direct phosphorylation relationship is NOT strongly evident in
bulk tumor phosphoproteomics. Possible explanations:

1. **Spatial Separation**: CDK7 (in TFIIH complex, nuclear) and CDK11B (various
   locations) may not co-localize in tumors despite co-expression

2. **Temporal Dynamics**: Phosphorylation may be transient or cell cycle-specific,
   averaged out in bulk tumor measurements

3. **Phosphatase Activity**: Strong phosphatase activity may counterbalance
   CDK7-mediated CDK11B_T595 phosphorylation in vivo

4. **Context-Dependency**: The in vitro observation may require specific co-factors
   (MAT1, cyclin H) or cellular conditions not universally present in tumors

5. **Alternative Kinases**: Other kinases may be the dominant CDK11B_T595 kinases
   in vivo, with CDK7 playing a minor or regulatory role

The SUBSTRATE-level coupling (CDK1_T161-CDK11B_T595, r=0.37) is STRONGER than
direct regulatory site coupling, suggesting functional coordination occurs through
SHARED PATHWAY ACTIVATION rather than direct phosphorylation.

================================================================================
LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

1. **Bulk Tumor Measurements**:
   - Data represents average across all cells in tumor sample
   - Misses cell-type-specific coupling (tumor cells vs stroma)
   - Misses subcellular compartmentalization
   - Averages across cell cycle phases

2. **Missing Data**:
   - BRCA and GBM excluded due to lack of phosphoproteomics data
   - CDK1_T161 not detected in PDAC
   - CDK12_T893 poorly detected (only 3/7 cancers)
   - Other CDK7 substrates (CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, CDK9) not analyzed due to
     insufficient detection

3. **Correlation Limitations**:
   - Correlation does not imply causation
   - Cannot distinguish direct phosphorylation from indirect co-regulation
   - Cannot detect non-linear relationships
   - Sensitive to outliers and sample size

4. **Protein-Normalized Phospho Data**:
   - Normalization by total protein removes coupling that operates through
     protein abundance changes
   - May obscure mechanisms where CDK7 activity affects CDK11B protein stability

5. **Sample Size Variation**:
   - OVARIAN has low sample sizes (n=83-10) - flagged as low confidence
   - Other cancers have n=97-108 (adequate)

6. **Isoform Ambiguity**:
   - CDK11A vs CDK11B not always distinguishable in mass spec data
   - CDK11B has multiple isoforms (p110, p58) with different functions
   - Phosphoproteomics cannot distinguish which isoform is phosphorylated

7. **Technical Considerations**:
   - Phosphosite detection bias (highly abundant/stable phosphosites favored)
   - Batch effects across different cancer studies
   - Variable tissue processing and sample quality

SUMMARY
================================================================================

CDK7 and CDK11 show MODERATE, CANCER-SPECIFIC coupling across CPTAC cancer
datasets. The strongest coupling occurs at the SUBSTRATE level (CDK1_T161-
CDK11B_T595, r=0.78 in HNSCC), while direct regulatory site coupling is weak.
Protein expression shows concordant upregulation in 57% of cancers, but
phosphorylation stoichiometry is independently regulated. The in vitro
CDK7→CDK11B_T595 phosphorylation observed in PhosphoSitePlus is not strongly
evident in bulk tumor data, suggesting context-dependent or spatially restricted
interaction.

Cancer-type-specific patterns suggest distinct CDK regulatory networks, with
squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC, LSCC) showing stronger multi-level coupling
than adenocarcinomas. This has potential therapeutic implications for targeting
CDK signaling in cancer.
